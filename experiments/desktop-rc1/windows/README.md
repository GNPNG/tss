# Windows RC.1 on the GTX 1650 Ti Max-Q

The packaged x64 editor opened the two-second song, keyed artwork and moving matte from the example directory. The Violet / Blue track used the original rear film and the 1,000 cSt front film. A private Windows desktop exercised the normal project dialogs and sent a mouse stroke to the native wheel surface. Saving and reopening restored the film choices and four named gestures. **Oil and Star slide pull** can be played manually. **Timed front oil turn** is bound to the song and contributes ten scheduled events to the export. Removing that take from the scheduler input reduces its output from twenty events to ten. [Project](oil-and-star.tss) · [Verification data](verification.json) · [Operator guide](../../../docs/windows-preview.md)

With the complete program playing at 640 × 360 on the GTX 1650 Ti Max-Q through Vulkan, the editor presented 390 frames over 10.426 s, averaging **37.41 FPS**. The 2 s song restarted four times within the measurement. The median of sampled rolling p95 frame intervals was 29.84 ms. [Timing samples](native-timing.json)

Native export produced 60 consecutive frames at 3840 × 2160 and 30 fps, with 8-bit Rec.709 H.264 video and two seconds of stereo 48 kHz AAC. The decoded soundtrack peaked at −15.1 dBFS. The final portable archive was extracted and its program and example files checked against the tested files. The optional WinGet installer stayed idle on this host. The app launched the FFmpeg and FFprobe 9.0.2 executables through their installed package targets. [Play the native movie](oil-and-star-4k.mp4)
