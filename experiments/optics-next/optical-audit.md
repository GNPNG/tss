# Optical diagnostic audit

The comparison images are flat and have sparse small droplets. This is a limitation of the experiment, not evidence that a real liquid projector behaves that way.

The existing `tracer.wgsl` assigns one output pixel per starting ray and never accumulates exiting rays at a screen. Refraction affects propagation through the next modeled cell, Fresnel transmission and the pupil cutoff, but there is no modeled lens or screen irradiance redistribution. The original output therefore cannot form physical focusing caustics on a screen. Its five fixed source directions and per-vessel image-plane shifts are only a crude approximation to illumination and defocus.

For the current shared-transfer examples, the assumed gap is 0.006 domain heights and the source grid height is 160: the gap is 0.96 source-grid cells. The assumed 35° meniscus model has center bulge `h/(2 cos θ) - h tan θ/2`, approximately 0.00156 domain heights or 0.25 source-grid cells. A one-cell Gaussian smoothing is applied to the signed-distance reconstruction. The phase interface is therefore modeled at a size smaller than one source-grid cell, while the entire 160 × 160 simulation is expanded to 3840 × 2160 output. This is not evidence of 4K fluid detail. Refractive indices 1.333 and 1.36, a pupil slope of 0.30, and source angular samples of 0 and ±0.065 are explicitly chosen parameters, not measured properties. Their effects should be examined with controlled tests and convergence, not inferred from saturation alone.

The per-vessel phase geometry is reconstructed from `rho > phi`; it is not a solved 3-D interface. Absorption coefficients come from artist-selected RGB transmittance, not measured spectra. A single sampled concentration is multiplied by phase lengths within each march step; there are 24 steps and at most one bracketed crossing per step. The model can miss multiple crossings, cannot trace internal reflections, and does not demonstrate conservation of reconstructed phase volume. The dye replay conserves its checked discrete budgets, but physical phase confinement and diffusion remain unvalidated. The hue and density multipliers cannot create missing sub-grid droplet structure.

## Screen-plane experiment

`screen-rays.wgsl` exports the central, collimated ray's exit position, transverse slope and linear RGB throughput for each source pixel. `screen_projection.py` advances those rays by a user-specified distance and conservatively splats their power onto a periodic detector with bilinear weights. The screen is a unit square in domain-height units; the plotted linear result is relative to a uniform straight-ray input, not calibrated irradiance. This is a forward power-deposition model: it is not a thin-lens/projector model, does not calculate finite-aperture depth of field or point-spread functions, and may require many more source rays per pixel to resolve converged caustics. Its default image clips irradiance above display white; the linear `.npy` output retains that data.

`test_screen_projection.py` verifies a uniform parallel bundle, an analytic converging bundle, periodic per-channel conservation and invalid input rejection without a GPU. These tests validate the deposition calculation in their specified cases, not the refraction shader, the boundary model or photorealistic fidelity. NVIDIA execution and actual-field comparison are tracked separately. No GPU work should be run on the affected Mac while the reported GPU execution/recovery issue is under investigation.

Remaining validation includes planar-interface Snell/Fresnel tests with non-equal refractive indices, analytically known lens or curved-interface focusing, convergence across source rays and output resolution, independent reference implementation, volume and interface-curvature checks, calibrated optical parameters, and comparison to measured or photographed transmitted light.

The first actual NVIDIA Vulkan export used the first two-vessel shared-transfer snapshot, 256 × 256 source rays, 24-row bounded dispatch, dye multipliers `2,2,2,1`, colors `f38ed0,29d4dd,6078d9,ffffff`, gap `0.006`, refractive indices `1.333,1.36`, and pupil slope `0.30`. The input field SHA-256 was `e1a5ac67c5135e63f70c81ef2e317376b69044d64cda882ecaca60ac6fe88249` for the complete 59-frame packed sequence; only its first frame was traced. The exported ray file SHA-256 was `f6611f75c78197df4f805f18c648231f66431f6d03a223edbd086ff0ee1b4e2a`. At an assumed propagation distance of 0.25 domain heights, a 256 × 256 periodic detector conserved the transmitted RGB sums to <5e-16 relative error in float64. However, 1,220 of 65,536 pixels received no sampled power and 2.79% exceeded display white before clipping. These are sampling/clipping artifacts, not demonstrated physical droplets or photorealistic caustics. See `output/windows-screen-d025-{clipped.png,linear.npy}` and `output/windows-screen-rays-256.f32le`. Higher source-ray counts against the same detector are being evaluated.

The subsequent bounded NVIDIA Vulkan sampling study traced 512² and 1024² source rays using the same first-frame conditions, and accumulated each onto the same 256² detector at distance 0.25. Zero-power pixels fell from 1,220 at 256² sampling to zero at 512² and 1024². Mean absolute linear-RGB change was 0.03630 from 256² to 512² and 0.01678 from 512² to 1024²; corresponding RMSE was 0.10074 and 0.03605. Maximum relative transmitted RGB energy error over the three runs was 9.01e-15 in float64. Display-white clipping affected 2.79%, 2.35% and 2.11% of pixels respectively. The decreasing difference does not prove convergence, and image appearance is still visibly unlike the reference. Full numeric results and ray hashes are in `output/windows-screen-convergence.json`. The actual clipped 1024²-ray image and unclipped linear detector array are `output/windows-screen-1024-d025-clipped.png` and `output/windows-screen-1024-d025-linear.npy`.

The 512² and 1024² raw ray files are currently transient at `/private/tmp/tss-windows-screen-rays-512.f32le` and `/private/tmp/tss-windows-screen-rays-1024.f32le`. Re-run the CPU deposition, without a GPU dispatch, as `python screen_projection.py RAYS NEW_OUTPUT_DIR --grid 1024 --detector-grid 256 --distance 0.25`. The source field data needed to repeat the GPU trace is also transient; hashes and parameters do not substitute for a durable input bundle.
