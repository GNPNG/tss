# Liquid-light reference and rendering research

The visual target is the appearance of a liquid light show as seen live, in clean projected light. The user requested photorealism, separately simulated color layers, and controls over their colors. Artificially aged footage, film damage and a generalized psychedelic animation are not the target. The user has rejected the existing procedural study as inauthentic.

## References and observed characteristics

- The supplied projected slide photograph shows a violet, blue, cyan and pale projection with rounded, uneven cells, narrow darker boundaries, small droplets, translucent overlaps and variation within the colored regions. The photograph is a still; it cannot establish the speed or motion of those forms. It is a visual reference, not a texture to be copied into the output.
- [Rotating slide demonstration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SqM5iv0OoCg) describes a liquid slide, including the violet/blue color option, and a rotating projected image. Its motion is a reference for a rotating wheel; the exact liquids, geometry and refractive indices are not specified there.
- [Joshua Light Show's *Liquid Loops*](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/200467), catalogued as 1967, is described by MoMA as an improvised manipulation of oil, water and glycerin, with multiple overlapping projections used in performances. The collection record concerns a film transfer, so its recorded image should not be taken as a calibrated measurement of the appearance of the live projection.
- [Dick Hogle's account](https://www.dickhogle.com/post/east-coast/) describes dyed oils and colored water/glycerin, his own overhead-projector trays, and the nested clock-crystal arrangement he later learned about. His account establishes physical arrangements and materials, not one universal historical recipe.
- The user also supplied a [video reference at 42 seconds](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fkv8hXnmKrQ&t=42s) and previously selected reference C. These are visual/motion references for review; their optical or material properties have not been measured here.

## Model implications

Each selectable color requires its own state and controls. A palette sampled from a single procedural field does not satisfy that requirement. A change in a color's optical properties should be separable from a change in its fluid state, and the operator needs to inspect each color's contribution independently.

An editable color, a dissolved dye and an immiscible liquid phase are distinct concepts. Multiple dyes may occupy the same liquid phase; oil and water are separate phases with an interface. Four independent dye or fluid simulations can provide independent editable fields, but do not by themselves enforce shared momentum, mutual displacement, interfacial tension or correct coalescence between phases. Likewise, separately composited projection sources need not be modeled as if all their liquids occupy the same vessel. The scene model must identify whether fields represent coupled phases in one vessel or separate projected vessels.

For a coupled multiphase candidate, evaluate phase fractions or phase fields, shared flow and pressure, density and viscosity, pairwise interfacial tension, contact angle and vessel boundary conditions. Dritselis and Karapetsas describe a volume-of-fluid approach with interfacial tension and contact angles for multiphase flows ([paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09870)); this is a research reference, not evidence that its solver has been adopted or is practical in real time on the target devices. Thin confined liquids and a rotating wheel versus manually manipulated plates imply different boundary geometry and forcing. Both need validation against motion references; an implementation must not label one generic motion field as both physical mechanisms without evidence.

The light path also matters. Absorption depends on wavelength, concentration and the length of the path through an absorbing material. RGB-channel exponential attenuation can approximate transmission, but RGB values do not uniquely specify a dye's absorption spectrum and this calculation is not calibrated spectral dye mixing. A full light model would also need to evaluate interfaces, refraction and illumination, where relevant, rather than asserting that a drawn dark contour is an optical simulation. See the wavelength-specific Beer–Lambert discussion in [this research review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8553265/). Quantitative material and projector parameters for the supplied reference are unknown.

## Optical focus and allocation of detail

The user proposes using out-of-plane focus in a projector or microscope as both a visible optical effect and a way to budget live rendering. Treat focus, aperture and the depth of each contribution as adjustable scene properties. Estimate each contribution's projected blur and use that estimate, together with its transmitted-light contribution and output scale, when selecting the detail needed for shading. Apply a soft depth-dependent defocus as part of the optical composition; compare it with reference footage rather than adding generalized aging or a uniform blur.

One starting model is a thin lens. For focal length `f`, object distance `s`, image distance `v = f*s/(s-f)`, aperture diameter `D`, and screen distance `v_focus` selected for the focal plane, the geometric defocus circle at the screen is `c = D*abs(1-v_focus/v)`. Convert `c` to output pixels using the projected screen scale. This is a candidate approximation for a specified optical layout, not a measurement of the supplied projector or a complete microscope model. Real lens aberrations, aperture shape, illumination, diffraction and the location of the dye within the optical train may require a different point-spread function or measured calibration. For the thin-lens construction and its depth-of-field geometry, see [Barsky et al., *Camera Models and Optical Systems Used in Computer Graphics*](https://graphics.berkeley.edu/papers/Barsky-CMO-2003-05/Barsky-CMO-2003-05.pdf).

Depth and transmittance must be considered before merging overlapping contributions; blurring a flattened final image cannot generally reproduce layers at different focal depths. Keep the resolution of sharp features and visible small droplets where needed, and measure the error and cost of changing quality as depth, focus or visibility changes. Reduced optical shading does not imply stopping or degrading a coupled fluid simulation. When layers become visible or move into focus, transitions must be smooth and their state continuous. The full-quality 4K output and sustained live performance both require independent verification.

## Comparison procedure

Use the supplied photograph for spatial and color comparison and time-based references for motion. Keep reference and candidate crops, aspect ratio, viewing conditions, color management and playback time explicit; do not infer a physical quantity solely from an uncalibrated image. For each candidate, record seed, resolution, frame rate, timestep, scene configuration and device.

- Inspect individual color fields with all others disabled, then compare the composite. Verify changes to one color's optical setting preserve the other fields and that independent motion and visibility are controllable. For coupled phases, separately test whether physically expected interactions respond to changes in other phases.
- Compare distributions of region and droplet size, contour smoothness and curvature, boundary thickness, overlap transmission and local brightness. Inspect multiple scales, including a 100% crop, to expose stair-stepping or loss of fine droplets. These are proposed measurements; no thresholds have yet been established from calibrated references.
- Compare multiple consecutive frames and longer sequences for gradual transport, deformation, coalescence and pinch-off where visible in the source. Record events and their timing rather than judging motion from a single frame. Confirm that numerical artifacts and repeated patterns are not mistaken for liquid behavior.
- Assess output at the intended preview and 4K dimensions, both at rest and with music, while recording actual sustained frame times and export throughput. Check supported hardware on Intel macOS and Windows/CUDA separately. A higher-resolution output frame does not itself prove a sufficiently resolved simulation.
- Present actual generated stills **and video** for the user's visual approval. Retain the settings and build used for each candidate so a reviewed result can be reproduced.

## Status

The application renderer is a procedural study, documented as such in the project README; it has not achieved the requested appearance or separate physical color simulation. An isolated four-field GPU fluid experiment has demonstrated independently evolving color fields, but its composite still has blocky contours and flat, uniform regions relative to the photograph. Its independent solvers and RGB optical attenuation are approximations, not a validated coupled immiscible-fluid and spectral projection model. It has not been integrated into the application, approved by the user, or verified for 4K output or sustained performance on the target systems.
