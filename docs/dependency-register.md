# TSS dependency and capability register

The [master library and demo catalog](library-catalog.md) compares the discovered methods by material, language/API, platform limits, source size, integration effort and observed results. The [sortable catalog](library-catalog.csv) supports filtering across those dimensions. The earlier capability evaluations below retain their original test context.

Status captured on 2026-11-19. This register maps the requested capabilities to evidence and candidate libraries; it is not a claim that the application is complete or that every candidate has been installed, selected, licensed for distribution, or validated. The [TSS epic](https://gitea.gnomatix.com/gnpng/org/issues/3) and issues [4](https://gitea.gnomatix.com/gnpng/org/issues/4), [5](https://gitea.gnomatix.com/gnpng/org/issues/5), [6](https://gitea.gnomatix.com/gnpng/org/issues/6), [7](https://gitea.gnomatix.com/gnpng/org/issues/7), [8](https://gitea.gnomatix.com/gnpng/org/issues/8), and [10](https://gitea.gnomatix.com/gnpng/org/issues/10) track the associated work. Any proposed replacement of an existing choice requires the project's functionality-equivalence review before adoption.

Do not introduce substantive project numerical, renderer, or process-control implementations in Python. Evaluate established native libraries and platform APIs for the required behavior and performance. A third-party tool's implementation language, API constraints and integration surface must be assessed explicitly rather than conflated with a project implementation choice.

**Status terms:** **App** means the dependency or behavior is present in the current application source; it is not a guarantee that both platforms or every input have been tested. **Experiment** means isolated from the app. **Candidate** means unselected and requiring evaluation. **Open** means no qualifying implementation or validation is established. Versions refer to inspected files, not a promise that later versions have identical features.

## Hardware and operating model

- The test Mac reports AMD Radeon Pro 5300M, 4 GB, and Intel UHD Graphics 630; the Windows test system reports NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1650 Ti, 4 GB. These are test devices, not model-name requirements for users. Runtime selection must enumerate devices and capabilities, and test actual workloads on each supported backend.
- Separate execution on devices is not simultaneous multi-GPU operation. No sustained 4K live rate, 4K fluid simulation, or approved photorealistic renderer has been established. Radeon GPU dispatches were stopped after reported timeout events; GPU jobs on Windows require coordinated scheduling, telemetry and documented device selection. Those controls do not guarantee driver recovery. See [GPU candidate evidence](gpu-fluid-candidates.md), [optical study](../experiments/optics-next/README.md), and [GPU job-control evidence](../experiments/gpu-job-control/README.md).
- A library's declared GPU support does not establish that its required multiphase solver, rendering engine, selected GPU, 4 GB memory budget, or a particular OS/driver combination works. Demonstrate and profile the actual paths on each machine; distinguish simulation resolution from video dimensions and offline throughput from live frame rate.

## Rendering and physics

The owner specifies that the reference is a stack of **distinct sandwiched slide layers**, like flattened lava lamps without a preferred in-plane direction, with bubble-like pockets in confined thin films, not an open pool. Each layer has its own simulation domain and material/color identities; bubbles may coalesce within that layer, but **layers and their bubbles never merge across layers**, even where their projections overlap. The through-stack optical axis defines the microscope-like plane of focus and the direction of compression. Combine the layers optically in depth order, calculate focus/defocus from layer depth and focal-plane settings, and map pressure to compression of the appropriate assembly/layers. Performance controls may target selected layers, and music or gestures may introduce in-plane motion without a permanent up/down bias. Accurate physics is required at mergers without modeling every interior particle. Investigate bulk/interface models, plate spacing, wetting, coalescence and transmission. A thin-gap approximation and its range of validity must be established for the chosen scene; the reference does not by itself establish constant thickness, a particular flow regime, or a verified solver.

### Fluid references supplied by the project owner

- [Wumpf's CFD notes](https://gist.github.com/Wumpf/b3e953984de8b0efdf2c65e827a1ccc3) survey SPH, hybrid methods, LBM and rendering; the author describes them as informal research notes with possible errors. [Blub](https://github.com/Wumpf/blub) is an MIT-licensed Rust GPU APIC study with a pressure solver and screen-space rendering. Its inspected scene and particle code do not establish coupled oil/water materials or interfacial tension. Its author closed the [Mac compatibility issue](https://github.com/Wumpf/blub/issues/26) without implementing Metal support. The locked upstream source [built on Windows](../experiments/blub-study/README.md); no GPU execution was performed. The application requests Vulkan and a high-performance adapter, but does not select a device by UUID; verify actual device selection and guarded execution before running it. These are source/build findings, not tests on the user's Radeon.
- [WebGPU-Ocean](https://github.com/matsuoka-601/WebGPU-Ocean) and [WaterBall](https://github.com/matsuoka-601/WaterBall), discussed by their author in the [WebGPU fluid simulations article](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/02/26/webgpu-fluid-simulations-high-performance-real-time-rendering/), are MIT-licensed WebGPU/TypeScript/WGSL examples using MLS-MPM and screen-space rendering; Ocean also offers an SPH mode. The author reports performance on a particular AMD integrated GPU and warns SPH may freeze on Mac. The inspected MLS-MPM particle and material code does not establish coupled oil/water phases or interfacial tension; neither targeted machine has been benchmarked with these examples.
- [PavelDoGreat's WebGL Fluid Simulation](https://github.com/PavelDoGreat/WebGL-Fluid-Simulation/tree/a2d292931f19d9b3b9f564e23e6c32729d2121c3) is MIT-licensed, uses GPU WebGL shaders, mouse and multitouch input, and separates simulation resolution from dye resolution. The [inspected source](https://github.com/PavelDoGreat/WebGL-Fluid-Simulation/blob/a2d292931f19d9b3b9f564e23e6c32729d2121c3/script.js#L882-L930) stores one velocity field and one RGB dye field; separate interacting oil/water phases and projector optics are not established. Its resolution split is worth evaluating against full-quality optical and fluid baselines, including the user's depth-of-field and occlusion requirements; it is not evidence those adaptive methods are implemented.
- [Haxiomic's GPU Fluid Experiments](https://github.com/haxiomic/GPU-Fluid-Experiments), supplied as its [HTML5 demo](https://haxiomic.github.io/GPU-Fluid-Experiments/html5/), is GPL-3.0 Haxe/Lime with GPU shader-based 2D fluid and particle simulation. Its inspected source has one velocity and pressure field with dye and particles carried by that flow. A `RenderTarget2Phase` is a ping-pong buffer, not evidence of two material phases. Its [quality presets](https://github.com/haxiomic/GPU-Fluid-Experiments/blob/master/Source/Main.hx) adjust particle count, fluid resolution and solver iterations based on frame rate, relevant to adaptive performance; the [monitor](https://github.com/haxiomic/GPU-Fluid-Experiments/blob/master/Source/PerformanceMonitor.hx) has an impossible `fps < 5 && fps > 180` condition, so its validation cannot be assumed. The author warns that the source may not build with present tools. Multiphase oil/water, projector optics, and distribution implications remain unverified; PavelDoGreat cites this project as a reference.
- [Evan Wallace's WebGL Water](https://madebyevan.com/webgl-water/), [source at `73eda8b`](https://github.com/evanw/webgl-water/tree/73eda8be832b649367b25ea5690c1f0181bb56ad), is an interactive GPU heightfield demonstration with reflections, refraction, soft shadows and a separate caustic texture. Its [author explains the planar-water caustics approximation](https://medium.com/@evanwallace/rendering-realtime-caustics-in-webgl-2a99a29a0b2c). Inspected source headers identify the code as MIT-licensed; third-party textures require separate rights review. Evaluate the approach for live optics, keeping its single-surface assumptions distinct from the required coupled fluids. No target-GPU execution or LuxCore integration has been established.
- [Cake23's fluid and pattern demonstration](https://cake23.de/turing-fluid.html) combines a GPU fluid simulation with advected reaction–diffusion-like pattern feedback. The author describes its patterns as a simpler one-component system rather than true Turing patterns, and specifies separate 512×256 fluid and 1024×512 pattern grids. It is a user-supplied visual and live-interaction reference for cellular effects and could be evaluated as an additional visual layer. Its inspected page does not state a source license; verify permissions and upstream components before reuse. Coupled oil/water interfaces, projector optics and target-GPU performance are unproven.
- [OpenFOAM multiphaseInterFoam](https://api.openfoam.com/2306/multiphaseInterFoam_8C.html) documents multiple incompressible fluids; [Ludwig](https://github.com/ludwig-cf/ludwig) documents binary and ternary phase-field models and GPU backends. Their phase behavior is relevant to evaluate. Neither source establishes that the required simulation runs on this Mac's AMD GPU, integrates with TSS, or achieves the requested interactive performance.
- **Confined-film candidates:** [Basilisk's Hele–Shaw solver](https://basilisk.fr/src/hele-shaw.h) is purpose-built for depth-averaged flow between close plates; its [GPU backend documentation](https://basilisk.fr/src/grid/gpu/grid.h) lists Metal, CUDA and others. Its supplied [Hele–Shaw example](https://basilisk.fr/src/examples/hele-shaw.c) alone does not demonstrate sharp immiscible interfaces, contact angles or changing plate spacing. [Ludwig's wall documentation](https://ludwig.epcc.ed.ac.uk/inputs/walls.html) covers binary and ternary wetting, and its [free-energy models](https://ludwig.epcc.ed.ac.uk/inputs/fe.html) describe multiphase interfacial behavior. Neither candidate establishes the required model running on both target GPUs or supports the requested plate-compression behavior without further investigation. Validate the chosen approximation against a suitable three-dimensional reference and keep distinct dye identities even where colors are not separate physical phases.

### CPU, GPU and LuxCore integration

- **Candidate path, unvalidated end to end:** SPlisHSPlasH exports particle VTK; the MIT-licensed native Rust [Splashsurf library and CLI](https://github.com/InteractiveComputerGraphics/splashsurf) document reconstructing SPH particles into triangle surfaces. [A Windows CPU test](../experiments/splashsurf-windows-study/README.md) reconstructed the upstream 4,732-particle SPlisHSPlasH fixture and passed Splashsurf's mesh checks; its 0.512-second single-run result is not a live benchmark. Reconstruct each required material without losing phase identity, verify interfaces and closed surfaces, and provide the resulting geometry to [LuxCore's C++ scene API](https://github.com/LuxCoreRender/LuxCore/blob/samples-v2.11.2/include/luxcore/luxcore.h), which supports defining meshes and separate materials. Whether the inspected solver supplies the required coupled interfacial physics remains open; surface reconstruction cannot supply missing physics.
- CPU simulation and mesh work can run alongside GPU optical rendering, subject to measured scheduling, transfer and memory costs. A verified GPU solver is another candidate where the actual phase model and device backend work. LuxCore's `PATHOCL` study selected NVIDIA OpenCL and used native CPU intersection threads; [RTPATHOCL](https://github.com/LuxCoreRender/LuxCore/blob/samples-v2.11.2/src/slg/engines/rtpathocl/rtpathocl.cpp) instead requires zero native rendering threads, which does not prevent separate CPU simulation work.
- LuxCore's [RANDOM/SOBOL sampling controls](https://github.com/LuxCoreRender/LuxCore/blob/samples-v2.11.2/samples/luxcoreui/samplerwindow.cpp) and [per-pixel importance channel](https://github.com/LuxCoreRender/LuxCore/blob/samples-v2.11.2/samples/luxcoreui/importancewindow.cpp) are potential tools for allocating optical samples. RTPATHOCL has its own preview/full-resolution reduction controls; support for the same importance-map mechanism there is not established. Test the user's focus/visibility heuristics against a full-quality baseline, including refracted and transmitted light, caustics and temporal stability. An apparently occluded or defocused layer may still affect visible light.
- LuxCore's API stops and restarts rendering for scene edits; deforming topology, per-frame CPU/GPU transfer, camera/pen interaction latency, deterministic replay and sustained 4K video have not been measured. Denoising is a candidate, not evidence of flicker-free animation. The target Mac Radeon and Windows NVIDIA must each be tested under the existing job-control constraints before asserting support.
- **Owner-directed live strategy:** pre-scan known audio and precompute timeline-driven simulation or reusable scene state where possible; buffer upcoming frames or intermediate work and use reduced internal rendering resolution and sampling for live playback. Incorporate live input promptly, invalidate or revise queued results as needed, record input with timestamps, and replay the performance for high-quality 4K output. Measure input-to-display latency, buffering delay, memory, cache invalidation, visual continuity and quality against an unoptimized reference; none of these behaviors is established by the library evaluations alone.
- **Owner-directed performance effects:** model the output as overlapping discrete layers, regions and blobs that can be addressed independently. Use their high-quality rendered plates or short cached sequences as inputs to GPU image operations for live movement, swirls, compression and adjustable focus/blur. Cache enough spatial, temporal, depth and transparency information for the chosen operations; route user input into controls for selected elements, and record/replay the same operations at export resolution. Where a control is explicitly mapped to physical simulation, replay its physical inputs as well and validate the result against the performed preview. Allocate live detail based on focus and visual contribution. Image-space transformations approximate effects on the existing render; validate depth-of-field, overlapping transmission, occlusions, boundaries and caustics against recomputed reference scenes. This does not establish that editing a plate changes the underlying coupled fluid physics.
- **Image-compositing candidates, not selections:** [PixiJS](https://pixijs.com/8.x/guides/components/filters) supplies scene objects and blur/displacement filters; its documented WebGPU backend is experimental. Native [Skia](https://api.skia.org/classSkImageFilters.html) exposes blur, displacement and blend filters. Evaluate GPU backend availability, adapter selection, renderer interoperation, color precision, transmission, 4 GB memory use and latency on both machines. A displacement filter alone does not establish a swirl control, physically correct optics or fluid coupling. The existing app uses separate procedural WebGL canvases; it does not implement the cached LuxCore plate approach.

### Video-feedback implementation candidates

- [ISF](https://docs.isf.video/primer_chapter_7.html) defines persistent image buffers, optional floating-point storage and multipass rendering; the documentation explicitly demonstrates feedback and transforms. Each proposed host must be tested for those features, cross-loop image routing, precision and reproducible scheduling; the format itself does not supply a complete performance interface or camera/mirror model.
- [VVISF-GL](https://github.com/mrRay/VVISF-GL) is a BSD-3-Clause C++ ISF implementation for GL environments, listed by the [ISF integration index](https://isf.video/integrations/) for macOS, Windows, Linux and iOS. Its repository requires building the shared libraries for the target SDK and compiler. Device selection, interoperation with the existing TSS renderer and the full feedback requirements are untested.
- [ISFMSLKit](https://github.com/mrRay/ISFMSLKit) renders ISF through Metal on macOS, with GLSL-to-Metal translation and included dependencies; Windows requires a separate host. Evaluate its dependency build, signing, licensing, persistent-buffer compatibility and actual Radeon behavior before considering it. The [Rust ISF library](https://github.com/nannou-org/isf) documents parsing and serialization, not a complete GPU renderer; an additional host would be required. The [JavaScript ISF renderer](https://github.com/msfeldstein/interactive-shader-format-js) is another host candidate whose platform and persistent-buffer behavior must be checked.
- [ossia score](https://ossia.io/score-docs/processes/shaders.html) documents ISF shaders and a graphics pipeline with multiple backends; its [distribution page](https://ossia.io/interactive-installations.html) identifies Windows/macOS/Linux and GPLv3. Evaluate it as an existing external performance tool or integration candidate, including interaction, loop routing, licensing and actual device behavior; it is not an established TSS component.

## Provenance and outstanding scope

The app is a development prototype. The isolated experiments and their generated output are evidence for specific tests, not proof that their code is integrated, visually authentic, production-ready, or approved. The listed candidates cover known requirement areas; full dependency closure, candidate selection, platform builds, license verification and implementation are still owed. Research and evaluation must preserve the user's existing library and architecture choices unless a separately reviewed functionality-equivalence case and authorization support a change.
