# Bill Ham and Light Sound Dimension

## How the liquid image was made

Bill Ham describes protecting the overhead projector’s Fresnel stage with clear glass, then placing concave clock crystals on it to hold liquid. Depth and curvature mattered to the operator. A deep crystal held more fluid. A round-bottomed crystal could spin, and Ham could stack several vertically. The overhead’s mirror and focusing lens projected the moving composition onto a wall or screen. Emi Ito’s photograph in the interview shows Ham demonstrating this arrangement to Kaishi Ito years later. [Ham’s interview with Ben Marks, June 2016](https://www.collectorsweekly.com/articles/lightman-fantastic/)

Ralph J. Gleason reported three projectors in Ham’s garage booth in January 1967. He observed the transparent dishes rocking, dipping and spinning while images faded between machines. The vessels on one stage and the beams arriving from different machines contributed two independently controllable kinds of overlap. In October 1967, Philip Elwood described Light Sound Dimension's Berkeley performance from the audience side of a translucent screen that filled the stage opening. Behind it the crew operated liquid, slide and film projection alongside spotlights and floods. [Gleason’s January 1967 report](https://billhamlights.com/reviews-by-ralph-j-gleason/) · [Elwood’s October 1967 report](https://billhamlights.com/reviews-by-philip-e-elwood/)

A contemporary advertisement names Bill Ham, Jerry Granelli and Fred Marshall at 1572 California Street in San Francisco. Ham’s archive identifies him and Bob Fine working backstage at the theater in a 1968 photograph. A separate gallery attributes storefront photographs and posters to Patrick McFarlin. [December 15, 1967 Berkeley Barb advertisement](https://s3.us-west-1.wasabisys.com/luminist/PR/BB_1967_12_15.pdf) · [1968 backstage photograph](https://billhamlights.com/history/) · [California Street theater gallery](https://billhamlights.com/lsd-theater/)

## TSS interpretation

In **Legends → Bill Ham**, audition two liquid overheads at the same screen position. Projector A starts at 100% relative light and focus 35%. B starts at 42% and focus 70%. Both begin at 3200 K. These are adjustable performance choices. With two visible oil tracks, rotate one slide, tug the other and fade the two beam intensities independently. The editor’s sealed, planar cells model a different vessel geometry from Ham’s curved, open clock crystals. [Rig definition](../../WebAssets/legends.js)

## Open historical parameters

The reviewed interviews, contemporary reports and exhibition records identify projector components and operators’ movements. They leave the overhead model, fitted focal length, aperture, throw distance, lamp code, measured lamp spectrum, dimmer response, clock-crystal radii, vessel spacing and liquid formulations unresolved for a specific Light Sound Dimension performance. The 2023 Haight Street Art Center exhibition included an overhead from Ham’s equipment, making its object label or a legible nameplate a concrete lead for identifying that specimen.
