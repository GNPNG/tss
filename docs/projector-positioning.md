# Positioning a physical projector for TSS

**One HD projector can present the full composition.** The Windows desktop editor renders virtual projectors A and B into a single program image, including their relative intensities, focus and registration. It opens one audience window on the first display Windows enumerates outside the editor's monitor. With only one display, it opens a regular window. The Mac build supports its native editor preview and a direct side-by-side stereo audience window on an external display. General delayed audience output on the Mac and independent feeds to multiple physical projectors remain future work. [Audience controls](../WebAssets/projector-output.js) · [Windows monitor selection](../src-tauri/src/live/windows/projector.rs) · [Platform support](../src-tauri/src/live.rs)

## One projector and one screen

1. **Measure the intended image.** Mark its left and right edges on the screen and measure their separation. Look up the throw ratio or zoom range for the installed lens and active aspect ratio. For ordinary direct projection, estimated throw distance equals **image width × throw ratio**. Use the manufacturer's diagram for the measurement datum, lens offset, shift limits and clearances. Folded and ultra-short-throw designs have their own placement drawings. [Epson distance and zoom tables](https://files.support.epson.com/docid/cpd6/cpd64535/source/setup/reference/projection_distance_pcls12000.html) · [Example lens-specific formulas and diagrams](https://files.support.epson.com/pdf/specs/specifications_ebpu2220series_en_r103.pdf)

   | Intended 16:9 image width | Image height | Estimated throw for a BenQ HT2060 (1.15–1.50:1) |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | 2.00 m | 1.125 m | 2.30–3.00 m |
   | 4.00 m | 2.25 m | 4.60–6.00 m |

   The HT2060 is a placement example with a native 1920 × 1080 DLP panel, 4LED illumination, a rated 2,300 ANSI lumens and 98% Rec.709 coverage. Confirm the mount and zoom against the 16:9 distance chart for the exact unit. [BenQ Canada specifications](https://www.benq.com/en-ca/projector/cinema/ht2060/spec.html) · [HT2060 installation manual](https://esupportdownload.benq.com/esupport/PROJECTOR/UserManual/HT2060/HT2060_UM_EN_230201102644.pdf)

2. **Set orientation and fit.** For front projection, place the projector on the audience side of a reflective screen. For rear projection, use a transmissive screen and set rear orientation so lettering reads correctly from the audience side. Select the ceiling orientation if the unit is inverted. Level the chassis, align it to the screen, and use optical zoom and lens shift for size and position. Let the optics stabilize before setting final focus. Use digital geometry correction only for residual alignment errors that the mount and optics cannot resolve. [Lens-shift and focus guidance](https://files.support.epson.com/docid/cpd6/cpd64932/source/projectors/source/basic_use/tasks/image_position_adjusting_lens_shift_no_dial.html)

3. **Check registration at panel resolution.** Extend the Windows desktop to the projector and keep the editor on the operator display. For a 1920 × 1080 panel, select that signal resolution, turn overscan off and show the linked PNG full-screen at 1:1 in an image viewer. Check the center crosshair against the marked center and all four corner IDs against the intended corners. Clipped marks indicate crop, masking or overscan. Inspect the one-pixel bars up close. Uneven widths or beat patterns can reveal resampling. For another native raster, use a plate made for that raster. [1920 × 1080 registration plate](calibration/registration-1920x1080.png)

4. **Check light and start the show.** From the intended viewing position, verify that black and near-black are distinct and the lightest gray is distinguishable from white. Match source and projector signal ranges. Use a neutral picture mode and stable white point. Disable automatic contrast or brightness adjustments that obscure faint film. In TSS select a live slide and click **Projector ↗**. The physical projector displays the finished mix. Leave any warm lamp color, creative offset or faint-film intensity already established in the rig applied once, in TSS.

## Matching a historical projection

Use a measured historical target where one exists. Record a neutral field, black, each isolated dye and a dye crossing from a known viewing position. For a measurable comparison, record screen luminance in cd/m² and chromaticity at matching positions in both installations. Repeat at the center, near the edges and from an off-axis seat. Measure the present unit on the intended screen in show lighting. White-level and grayscale adjustments can bring in-range targets closer. A target below the measured black floor, above peak luminance or outside the available color gamut sets a limit on the match.

Two projectors can produce the same perceived color at a reference seat with different light spectra. Matching the original spectrum through physical dyes or reproducing its appearance through another camera requires spectral measurements. Archive photos and film transfers alone leave the original absolute brightness and spectral response unresolved. For a research tribute, record which values were measured, which features were matched visually and which settings were chosen for performance.

## Positioning multiple physical projectors

A physical recreation would give each machine its own feed. On a setup with independent sources, label display outputs and projector inputs **A** and **B**. Mark each projector's lens position, throw, height, aim, screen and intended image corners. For two beams over the same screen, align each to the same marked rectangle with the other shuttered. For separate screen regions, mark each rectangle independently. Check one feed at a time with an identifying plate. A splitter or mirrored desktop repeats the finished picture on both machines. TSS's current audience output supplies that composite. Separate, synchronized per-projector rendering and output selection are required to drive an independent A/B rig directly from TSS.

Match white point and neutral gray from the chosen viewing position, and check the black floor with each physical projector on its own and with both on. For a rig calling for B at 8% of A, use isolated test fields with a luminance meter at the same screen patch, subtracting each channel's black reading. Set the intended ratio in one place. If TSS already scales B to 8%, calibrate the physical channels to equal reference output. Reapplying 8% physically would reduce B to approximately 0.64% in a linear system. Record the measured result, screen material, ambient light and observer position so the rig can be set up again.

**Intentional additive overlap** lets two different images illuminate the same patch. Leave edge blending off for that effect. **Tiled projection** divides one wide image across neighboring projectors. It uses complementary fades along the shared border and may need black-level compensation where projector leakage adds. Align geometry before adjusting those fades. [Edge blending](https://files.support.epson.com/docid/cpd6/cpd64101/source/adjustments/tasks/multi_projector_tasks/multi_projector_edge_blending.html) · [Black-level matching](https://files.support.epson.com/docid/cpd6/cpd61020/source/adjustments/tasks/multi_projector_tasks/multi_projector_black_level_pl1000u_1505u.html)

These measurements describe the hardware in your room. Keep them with the installation notes. The historical pack can retain the documented venue geometry, unresolved dimensions and artistic beam settings across installations. ## Trying stereo on a 3D television

On the Mac, connect the TV as an extended display. Open a project containing a visible live oil layer with an assigned depth, or choose a slide recipe in the editor. Select **Live 3D pressure** and set the TV to **3D → Side by Side**. The TV shows your project’s current dyes, layer order and evolving oil. Each stereo frame uses the same simulation state for both eyes. The Mac shows the left-eye view for aiming.

**Pressure target** below the Mac preview names the layer that will deform. Stereo starts with the frontmost visible depth-assigned oil layer. Select another visible live oil track in the timeline to press that layer. Place the pointer over one of its dye boundaries in the Mac preview, press the built-in trackpad, then gradually ease off. **Trackpad force** shows the measured force. **Applied slide load** shows the value sent to the targeted slide after the sensitivity curve.

At **1× sensitivity**, applied load equals measured force. For a more pronounced difference between light and hard presses, try **0.5×**. A 50% press then applies 25% load and a full press still applies 100%. For lighter touch, **2×** maps a 25% press to 50% load. Full load reduces the modeled gap by 65% at the center of contact. The display statistics retain peak force and a count of native pressure events.

**Stereo depth** adjusts the separation of the two eye views. The default is 20%. Move it to 0% for flat projection or toward 100% to exaggerate differences between slide planes. The Mac remembers stereo depth and trackpad sensitivity independently across sessions. **Stop live 3D** returns to the ordinary project preview.

**Resolution per eye** defaults to 960 × 540. Choose 640 × 360 for a lighter rendering load. Both settings fill the same TV output. The Mac remembers the stereo resolution separately from the editor’s ordinary preview resolution.

For an HDMI-connected television with half-side-by-side support, play the 1920 × 1080 SBS test full-screen on the television. Each half contains 960 × 1080 samples. The TV expands each half to the screen width and delivers it to the corresponding eye. Select **3D → Side by Side** on the television, put on its compatible glasses, and check the eye labels individually. If the labels are reversed, use the TV’s left/right swap. The computer sends ordinary video timing. The TV model and chosen HDMI input determine which timings and menu options are available. [Samsung F5500 series e-manual, 3D Mode](https://org.downloadcenter.samsung.com/downloadfile/ContentsFile.aspx?CDSite=UNI_CA&OriginYN=N&ModelType=N&ModelName=PN60F5500AF&CttFileID=5379280&CDCttType=UM&VPath=UM%2F201308%2F20130813133020082%2F%5BENG_US%5DX12ATSCF-1.119.pdf)

The [stereoscopic venue study](../experiments/historical-venue-study/README.md) puts the physical screen at the convergence distance. Its two backstage views reveal the distances between the projectors and platforms. The oil pressure study uses a different convention for artistic effect. It sets the middle slide at the screen plane and gives the front and rear slides opposite disparities.
